# Hair Texture Chart

This table is extracted from the corresponding Face Form Finder page for reuse as a plain-text reference. Read the accompanying explanation at [faceformfinder.com/hair-texture-chart.html](https://faceformfinder.com/hair-texture-chart.html).

| Observation band | How to recognise it | One strand of hair | A whole area of hair | What it can mean for everyday handling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fine strands | A clean, shed strand feels light between finger pads and can be hard to notice against a plain card without close attention. | The individual fibre feels delicate rather than wire-like. Fine describes thickness, not how much hair you have. | You may still have a full-looking head of hair if many fine strands grow in each area. | Use gentle handling when your own strands tangle or snap easily, and judge added weight by how your hair behaves rather than by the label alone. This is a general practice, not a product rule. |
| Medium strands | A strand is easy to locate and feel, but it does not feel unusually resistant or wire-like. | The fibre sits between the fine and coarse descriptions in your own comparison. | Density can still be low, medium, or high; medium strand thickness does not predict scalp coverage. | Change only one part of a routine at a time when testing results. The useful response is your hair’s response, not a promise attached to this band. |
| Coarse strands | A clean, shed strand is easy to feel between fingers and visibly reads as more substantial against a plain card. | The individual fibre can feel more robust or wire-like than other hairs from your own head. | A coarse strand can occur in a sparse-looking or a very full-looking area; strand thickness and density remain separate observations. | Work carefully if rough pulling causes friction or snagging in your own hair. That is general handling advice, not a claim that all coarse hair needs the same routine. |
| Lower density | When you part hair in several ordinary locations, more scalp is readily visible between growing hairs under the same light. | No single strand proves lower density. A strand may be fine, medium, or coarse. | The spacing between hairs can make the scalp more visible, especially at a part or crown. | Choose placement and parting with your own coverage in mind, and avoid treating a hair-type name as evidence about density. This is a general planning practice. |
| Medium density | With a normal part in ordinary light, scalp is visible at the part but surrounding areas do not immediately appear widely spaced. | No single strand defines this band. | The head can look balanced in coverage while still having fine, medium, or coarse individual strands. | Observe roots and ends separately before changing your routine; the density label does not prescribe a product or a haircut. |
| Higher density | Across several normal partings, many hairs occupy an area and the scalp is less readily visible away from the direct part. | No single strand defines this band. | A larger amount of hair in an area can create more visible bulk even when individual fibres are fine. | Section hair when it makes washing, drying, or detangling more manageable for you. This is a general technique, not a requirement for every dense head of hair. |
