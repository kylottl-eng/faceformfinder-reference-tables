# Nose Shape Chart

This table is extracted from the corresponding Face Form Finder page for reuse as a plain-text reference. Read the accompanying explanation at [faceformfinder.com/nose-shape-chart.html](https://faceformfinder.com/nose-shape-chart.html).

| Common label | How to recognise it | What to observe | How it differs from a nearby label | Observation meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Straight bridge | In side view, the bridge reads as a largely even line from upper bridge toward the tip. | Use a relaxed side view and follow the bridge line rather than judging the tip alone. | Unlike a convex bridge, there is no clearly forward-rounded section along the line; unlike a concave bridge, there is no obvious inward dip. | Keep bridge profile as one note and tip direction as another; a straight bridge does not define the whole nose. |
| Convex or Roman bridge | In side view, the bridge reads as more outwardly rounded or projected along part of its line. | Observe the bridge from a level side view in even light. | Unlike a straight bridge, the line has a more visible outward curve; this says nothing by itself about width or tip direction. | Record bridge profile separately from front-view width and tip features. |
| Concave or ski-slope bridge | In side view, the bridge reads with a visible inward dip before it reaches the tip area. | Look at the continuous side outline, not a close wide-angle image. | Unlike a straight bridge, the line visibly dips; unlike an upturned tip, this label describes the bridge line rather than tip direction. | Use a separate note for the tip; bridge contour and tip direction can coexist in many combinations. |
| Button-like tip | The tip reads compact and rounded relative to the bridge and surrounding features. | Compare the tip’s visible outline from a level front and a relaxed side view. | Unlike an upturned tip, compactness is the observation here; the tip does not need to point noticeably upward. | Keep tip size impression separate from nostril visibility and bridge profile. |
| Upturned tip | The tip direction reads upward in side view, and more nostril underside may be visible from the front. | Compare the tip direction with the bridge on a level side view. | Unlike a button-like tip, this label is about direction rather than compactness; both labels can apply together. | Record direction independently from width and bridge contour. |
| Downturned tip | The tip direction reads lower than the bridge line in a relaxed side view. | Use a level side image or mirror view with the head upright. | Unlike a convex bridge, this label describes the direction at the tip, not the contour along the bridge. | Keep tip direction as a separate observation; it does not set overall width or bridge shape. |
| Wider front view | From the front, the base or side-to-side outline reads broader relative to the rest of the nose. | Use a level front view in even light and compare the base outline, not one shadow. | Unlike a prominent bridge, this describes front-view width rather than apparent projection in side view. | Write width and side-profile observations separately because one does not determine the other. |
| Narrower front view | From the front, the bridge or base reads more contained side to side. | Check a level front view with the face relaxed and camera at normal distance. | Unlike a straight bridge, this describes width in front view rather than the bridge line in profile. | Keep the front-view note independent from tip direction and bridge contour. |
| Mixed features | Several labels apply together, such as a straight bridge with an upturned tip or a convex bridge with a wider front view. | Record bridge line, tip direction, tip outline, and front-view width as separate observations. | This is not a failed classification: the labels refer to different visible features and can overlap. | A combined description is often clearer than choosing one label for the entire nose. |
