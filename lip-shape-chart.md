# Lip Shape Chart

This table is extracted from the corresponding Face Form Finder page for reuse as a plain-text reference. Read the accompanying explanation at [faceformfinder.com/lip-shape-chart.html](https://faceformfinder.com/lip-shape-chart.html).

| Common label | How to recognise it | What to observe | How it differs from a nearby label | Observation meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fuller upper lip | The upper lip’s visible vertical area reads more substantial than the lower lip’s at rest. | Compare upper and lower lip separately with the mouth relaxed and closed gently. | Unlike balanced fullness, the upper and lower lip do not read equally substantial. | Record this as an upper-lip note; it does not describe the mouth width, corners, or lip outline. |
| Fuller lower lip | The lower lip’s visible vertical area reads more substantial than the upper lip’s at rest. | Compare the lower lip’s central area with the upper lip in even light. | Unlike fuller upper lip, the greater visible area is below rather than above. | Keep lower-lip fullness separate from the shape of the upper edge. |
| Balanced fullness | Upper and lower lips read similar in visible vertical area when relaxed. | View the mouth straight on with lips resting naturally rather than pressed together. | Unlike upper- or lower-dominant descriptions, neither lip consistently reads more substantial. | This is only a relative upper-to-lower observation, not a measure of overall mouth width. |
| Defined Cupid’s bow | The upper lip’s centre has two visibly rising peaks with a clear central dip between them. | Look only at the upper edge from the front in even light. | Unlike a soft upper edge, the peaks and centre dip read more distinct. | Record the upper-edge outline independently from upper-lip fullness. |
| Soft Cupid’s bow | The upper lip’s centre reads as a gentler curve, with less sharply separated peaks. | Observe the upper edge without stretching the mouth into an expression. | Unlike a defined Cupid’s bow, the centre transition looks less angular or pointed. | This describes the upper-edge contour, not the lip’s relative fullness. |
| Wider mouth outline | The mouth’s horizontal span reads broader across the face in a relaxed front view. | Look at the distance between the corners with the face level and the mouth at rest. | Unlike fuller lips, this describes side-to-side span rather than vertical visible area. | Write width as its own observation because it does not determine upper or lower fullness. |
| Narrower mouth outline | The mouth’s horizontal span reads more contained across the face in a relaxed front view. | Use a level front view; avoid a close wide-angle photo that changes apparent proportions. | Unlike a soft Cupid’s bow, this describes total span rather than the upper-edge shape. | Keep horizontal outline separate from both lip fullness and corner direction. |
| Upward-reading corners | The outer corners read slightly higher than the centre line of the mouth at rest. | Observe the mouth relaxed, without deliberately lifting the corners. | Unlike a defined Cupid’s bow, this concerns the outer corners, not the centre of the upper lip. | Record corner direction independently from width and upper-edge contour. |
| Downward-reading corners | The outer corners read slightly lower than the centre line of the mouth at rest. | Check the mouth in a neutral expression and ordinary light. | Unlike lower-lip fullness, this describes the direction at the corners, not lower-lip area. | Keep this separate from the size or outline of either lip. |
| Combined upper and lower features | Upper and lower lips, edges, corners, and width can each carry different descriptions at the same time. | Write separate notes for upper fullness, lower fullness, upper edge, corners, and mouth span. | This is not a failed classification: each label refers to a different visible feature. | A short combined description is usually clearer than choosing one label for the entire mouth. |
